Repair, Replacement or Upgrade? They Are Three Different Jobs

Panel repair, panel replacement and a service upgrade get confused constantly, and each answers a different question. Mixing them up is how homeowners end up overpaying — buying a replacement when a repair would do, or a bigger panel that adds no capacity at all.

The detail that trips people up

A new panel and more electrical capacity are not automatically the same thing. A 200-amp panel installed on a service that was never upgraded to supply 200 amps does not give you 200 amps. The panel is the distribution point. The service — the conductors and equipment feeding it from the meter — is what sets the ceiling. Any quote that treats "new panel" and "more power" as one item deserves a second look.

Repair: fix the fault, keep what works

Repair means fixing a specific fault — a breaker, a connection, a damaged component — without touching equipment that is still doing its job. If the panel and its busbar are in good condition, most single-symptom problems land here.

Replacement: when the equipment itself is the problem

Replacement is for equipment that is damaged, obsolete, or no longer safe to keep in service: rust or corrosion that has reached the breakers or busbar (common on outdoor Plant City panels), heat damage, or an old fuse box in a home built before the 1980s. The finding comes from opening the panel up, not from its age alone.

Upgrade: a capacity project, planned as one

An upgrade — most often 100A to 200A — can involve the service conductors, the meter base, coordination with the utility, plus a permit and inspection. It is worth planning as its own project rather than an add-on to a repair, and the starting point is a load calculation: adding up what the home actually draws, not guessing from the panel's age.
